v0.6.0 – Key rotation, metadata privacy, group calls

The room key now follows the room, the relay no longer learns room names
or display names, calls work for groups, and messages reach contacts who
are offline. Completes Phases C to F of the roadmap.

Added:
- Room key rotation on every change of membership: one member, chosen
  the same way by every client, draws a new generation and seals it to
  each member's identity key. A newcomer cannot read what came before;
  a member who left cannot read what comes after.
- Chat sealed under per-epoch keys derived from the current generation;
  during a change receivers hold two generations, so nothing in flight
  is lost.
- Group voice and video with a key per sender, authenticated
  announcements and a replay window per sender; names under the video
  tiles.
- Offline inbox on the relay: mail for a contact who is offline waits
  sealed, under an address only the two of them can compute. Retention
  is the operator's choice (--inbox 30d | Nh | off, 30 days by default).
- Optional TLS to the relay (--tls, --tls-pin; --tls-cert, --tls-key).
- Noise suppression and microphone sensitivity; manual video settings.
- Unread counts in the chat list.
- Optional direct calls via STUN, off by default: they reveal your IP
  address to the people you call.
- fear_admin, a Qt utility for the relay's database on the same machine.
- "How F.E.A.R. works", a short illustrated overview in English and
  Russian, next to the manual.

Changed:
- The relay sees a hash instead of the room name and a random tag per
  connection instead of the display name; names travel inside the
  encryption, signed together with the tag. Personal chat identifiers
  are derived under the pair's key.
- The desktop identity key is encrypted at rest (libsecret, DPAPI).
- One fingerprint per key on every platform (BLAKE2b, 8 bytes).
- One desktop window; closing it hides it to the tray.
- Release archives carry everything the GUI starts – calls, updater,
  key-exchange – in the layout the updater expects, with the manual and
  license texts. The Windows archive is now fear-windows-x86_64-*.zip,
  the name the updater looks for.
- License: AGPL-3.0-or-later for the relay and console client,
  GPL-3.0-or-later for the clients, instead of MIT.

Fixed:
- The relay sent media into chat connections and back to the sender;
  video round-trip time in a three-device test fell from a 703 ms
  median to about 70 ms.
- A member who joined by key exchange could be skipped as the rotator,
  leaving the room without a new key.
- A quiet call did not stop when asked; SIGTERM now stops a call
  cleanly, wiping its keys and releasing the camera.
- The search box above the chat list filters it; since 0.5.0 it
  accepted text and did nothing.

Security:
- Contact lists on the relay can be read only by their owner (a signed
  one-shot challenge).
- A modal warning when a peer's identity key changes.
- The GUI starts its programs only from its own directory, never from
  the working directory or PATH.

Known limitations:
- No post-compromise security (no ratchet): whoever obtains a room key
  reads that room until the next rotation.
- The room hash has no secret in it; common room names can be guessed.
- The relay still sees IP addresses, timing and volume.
- NAT traversal is a single STUN candidate, not yet on Android.

Downloads: fear-linux-x86_64-v0.6.0.zip (Ubuntu 22.04 and newer, Debian
12 and newer), fear-windows-x86_64-v0.6.0.zip; relay image
ghcr.io/shchuchkin-pkims/fear-server:v0.6.0. Archives are built and
signed by CI; the updater refuses anything not signed with the key in
updater/updater.conf.

Compatibility: not compatible with 0.5.x – rooms, names, chat frames
and calls all changed on the wire. Update every client and the relay
together.
